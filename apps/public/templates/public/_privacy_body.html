What we keep, and why

Your account

    {% if keeps_email %}
  • Your email address, to identify your account, to send you sign-in codes and to send the email alerts you turn on.
    • {% else %}
  • A key made from your email address: a one-way code that finds your account each time you sign in. Your address itself is never stored: it is used to send the sign-in email, then discarded.
    • {% endif %}
  • A masked address: the first letter and the domain, such as h•••@example.com, so you can see which account you're signed in to.
  • Sign-in requests: {% if keeps_email %}the address{% else %}the key and the masked address{% endif %}, when you asked to sign in, and whether you did. The code and link are stored only as one-way hashes. Requests are deleted once they expire, within a day.
  • Passkeys: a public key for each one you add, its name, and when it was last used. Your device keeps the private key; we never see it, or your face or fingerprint.
  • When your account was made and when you last signed in.

Why: to provide the service you signed up for (the lawful basis is our contract with you).

Looking up a flight

When you ask where a flight is, on the website or from Claude or ChatGPT, we send the flight number, and nothing about you, to Flightradar24, and keep its answer for about a minute so that several people asking about the same flight share one lookup. We don't record who asked. Our server logs may note the flight number, without your account.

Flights you follow, and alerts

If you follow a flight to be told when it takes off and lands, we keep the flight number, which alerts you chose (push, email or both) and when each alert was sent, until the day after it lands. If you turn on notifications on a device, your browser gives us a push address for that device, with its keys, and we keep it, with the browser's name, until you turn notifications off or the push service tells us it no longer works. Push messages are delivered by your browser's push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla), which sees that a message was sent to your device{% if keeps_email %}; email alerts go through our email provider{% endif %}.

Why: to send the alerts you asked for (contract). Your device asks for your permission before it lets us send push notifications, and you can withdraw it in your browser or device settings at any time.

Sharing a live link to a flight

If you share a flight, we keep the link's random code, the flight it shows, when it was made and when it ends. Anyone with the link can see that flight's live position, route and times, without signing in. The page doesn't show your name or email address. A link stops working 24 hours after the flight lands, or sooner if you stop sharing it from your account page; we then delete it.

Why: to show the flight to the people you chose to send the link to (contract). Please share only your own flights, or ones you have the traveller's agreement to share.

Connecting Claude or ChatGPT

When you connect an AI assistant, we keep a record of the connection: the app's name, the access it was given and when, and the tokens that let it act for you. To end a connection, remove {{ pwa.name }} in the assistant's settings; deleting your account ends them all. We receive only what the assistant sends to {{ pwa.name }}'s tools, such as a flight number; we never see the rest of your conversation. What you say to the assistant is handled by its maker, Anthropic or OpenAI, under their own privacy policy.

Why: to let the assistant you connected use your account (contract).

Keeping the service safe

Our servers log each request: your IP address, the date and time, the page asked for (without any query), the response and your browser's description of itself. We also count recent requests by IP address to stop abuse, and keep a short record of recent changes so that a repeated request isn't applied twice. Logs are kept by our hosting provider for a short time, normally no more than 30 days; the counts and records are deleted within a day.

Why: to keep {{ pwa.name }} secure and working (our legitimate interest in protecting the service and the people who use it).

Why: to understand how {{ pwa.name }} is used so we can improve it (legitimate interest), in a way that doesn't identify you.

Cookies and your device

We set two cookies, both strictly necessary, so they don't need your consent: one keeps you signed in (for up to 90 days, or until you sign out), and one protects forms from forgery. There are no analytics, advertising or third-party cookies.

The installed app stores pages you've opened and anything you do while offline on your device, so it works without a connection. Signing out removes them.

We may delete an account that hasn't been used for two years, after telling you if we can.
